How to Set a Grocery Budget and Stick to It

Easy Budget Tracker guides · Updated November 20, 2026

Groceries are one of the biggest flexible costs in a household budget, which makes them one of the best places to save without giving up much. A good grocery budget is realistic, planned in advance and easy to check while you shop.

1. Start from what you really spend

Add up your grocery receipts, or your bank and e-wallet records, for the last one to three months. The average is your starting number. Setting a figure far below it usually fails within a couple of weeks.

2. Turn the monthly amount into a weekly one

A weekly limit is easier to follow than a monthly one, because you see it run out in time to change course. A monthly budget of 6,000 works out to roughly 1,400 per week. If you shop twice a week, split it again.

3. Plan meals before you shop

  1. Choose a few simple dinners for the week and reuse leftovers for lunch.
  2. Check what you already have in the fridge and pantry.
  3. Write a list of only what is missing.

4. Shop with a list and a limit

Group your list by store section (fruit and vegetables, meat and fish, dairy, pantry, household, personal care) so you walk the shop once and skip impulse aisles. Keep a running total as you go, and eat before you shop, because hungry shoppers spend more.

5. Compare unit prices

The bigger pack is not always cheaper. Compare the price per kilo, litre or piece, and buy staples in bulk only if you will use them before they spoil.

6. Record the total and learn from it

After each trip, note the amount. At the end of the month, compare it with your budget. If you went over, look at which items caused it and decide whether to change the habit or the budget.

Small habits that add up

  • Keep a short list of backup meals that cost little, such as eggs, rice and vegetables.
  • Check dates and discounts on items you already buy regularly.
  • Do not shop without a list, even for a quick visit.
