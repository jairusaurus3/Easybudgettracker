How to Make a Monthly Budget in 5 Steps

Easy Budget Tracker guides · Updated November 20, 2026

A monthly budget is a plan for the money you expect to receive and how you will use it before the month starts. It does not have to be complicated. Five steps are enough for most people.

Step 1: Write down your take-home income

Use the money that actually reaches you after tax and deductions. If you also have side income or allowances, list them separately. If your pay changes a lot from month to month, start with the lower amount you can count on, and see our guide on budgeting on an irregular income.

Step 2: List your fixed bills

Fixed bills are the payments that are the same or nearly the same each month: rent or mortgage, electricity and water, internet, phone, insurance and subscriptions. Write the amount and the due date of each one. The monthly bills checklist can help you remember the ones that are easy to forget.

Step 3: Estimate your variable spending

Groceries, transport, eating out and shopping change every month. Look at the last one or two months of receipts, bank or e-wallet history and use those numbers as your starting estimate. If you have no records yet, track your spending for a month first (see how to track your expenses) and then set the limits.

Step 4: Pay your savings first

Decide on a savings amount before you decide how much to spend. Even a small fixed amount counts. Treat it like a bill that is paid on the day your income arrives. If you do not have an emergency fund yet, that is the first place to send it.

Step 5: Check what is left and adjust

Add up bills, spending and savings and subtract the total from your income. The result should be zero or more. If it is negative, trim the flexible categories first, such as eating out and shopping, until the plan fits.

A worked example

ItemPlanned amount
Take-home income30,000
Fixed bills (rent, utilities, internet, phone)12,000
Groceries6,000
Transport2,500
Eating out and fun3,000
Savings4,500
Buffer for surprises2,000
Total planned30,000

The amounts above are only an illustration. Use your own numbers and your own currency.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting yearly costs. Insurance, school fees and holiday gifts are easy to miss. Divide them by 12 and set that amount aside each month.
  • Making categories too tight. A budget you break in the first week does not help. Leave a small buffer.
  • Never looking at it again. Check your remaining balance once a week and adjust early.
