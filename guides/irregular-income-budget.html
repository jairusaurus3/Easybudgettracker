How to Budget on an Irregular Income

Easy Budget Tracker guides · Updated November 20, 2026

Freelancers, commission earners, drivers, small business owners and seasonal workers often do not know their exact income until it arrives. A normal budget still works, but it needs a safer starting point and a bigger buffer.

1. Find your baseline

Look at your income for the last six to twelve months and find your lowest typical month. That is your baseline, not the average. Planning around the average means a slow month will leave you short.

The average here is about 20,800, but the lowest month is 14,000. A budget built on 14,000 to 15,000 will hold in every one of these months.

2. Cover the must-pay bills first

List rent, utilities, food, transport and minimum payments, and make sure the baseline covers them. If it does not, that is the number to work on first, either by lowering costs or by raising your minimum income.

3. Pay yourself a steady amount

Keep income in one place and move a fixed amount to your spending account on the same day each month, like a salary. In good months the extra stays behind. In slow months you draw on it.

4. Give extra money a job

When a month brings in more than the baseline, split the extra on purpose:

  • First, your buffer until it covers one to three months of must-pay costs.
  • Then savings goals and any extra payments you want to make.
  • If you are self-employed, set aside a share for taxes. Check the rules where you live.

5. Review every month

At the end of each month, enter what you actually earned and spent, see what carried over, and adjust next month. Over time you will see your real pattern and can raise the baseline.

In Easy Budget Tracker you can enter a different income amount for each month, and leftover money can carry over to the next month, so a good month helps a slow one. A bigger safety net is explained in our guide on building an emergency fund.
